Global Health Workforce & SDG Progress Analysis

Combined public datasets from the UN, WHO, World University Rankings, and Worldometer to explore how healthcare workforce density, economic development, and tertiary education relate to health outcomes across countries.

Download Presentation View Notebook

Overview

Health outcomes are shaped by more than healthcare services alone. Economic capacity, public health expenditure, education infrastructure, and healthcare workforce availability can all influence whether countries are able to build sustainable and equitable health systems. This project used Python to source, clean, combine, and analyse public datasets from a policy analytics perspective.

My Contribution

I contributed across the full project lifecycle, with a particular focus on Python-based data wrangling and analysis for the first two research questions. I also proposed the limitation and web scraping extension, which reframed the project from a static 2017 cross-country analysis into a potential real-time monitoring solution.

Problem Context

Public health and policy decision-makers often need to understand how health outcomes relate to workforce capacity, education infrastructure, and economic conditions. However, these indicators are usually stored across separate public datasets, making it difficult to analyse them together without data sourcing, auditing, cleaning, and integration.

Research Questions

Question 1

How does medical staff density differ between regions, and how does it relate to health and lifespan?

The analysis explored whether countries and regions with higher medical staff density also showed stronger life expectancy and lower infant mortality outcomes.

Question 2

What is the relationship between different types of medical staff density and economic indicators?

The analysis compared medical doctors, nurses, and midwives density with GDP per capita, urban population, health expenditure, and related economic indicators.

Question 3

How does tertiary education differ across countries, and does it relate to medical staff density?

The analysis considered whether tertiary education enrolment and university infrastructure were associated with healthcare workforce capacity.

Question 4

How could real-time public indicators support continuous monitoring of health and SDG progress?

This question could not be answered by the 2017 combined dataset alone, so a web scraping extension was proposed to collect real-time indicators with timestamps.

Key Insights

Healthcare workforce density was associated with better health outcomes.

Countries with higher medical staff density tended to show stronger life expectancy and lower infant mortality, suggesting a relationship between workforce availability and health system performance.

Economic capacity helped explain workforce differences.

GDP per capita, urbanisation, and health expenditure were relevant when comparing medical workforce density across countries.

Tertiary education may support long-term workforce capacity.

Education infrastructure and tertiary enrolment provide a useful lens for understanding how countries develop future healthcare professionals.

Static annual datasets are not enough for monitoring.

A single-year dataset can support cross-country comparison, but it cannot provide continuous monitoring, anomaly detection, or timely escalation.

Limitation & Web Scraping Extension

The combined dataset was useful for cross-country comparison, but it was limited to annual 2017 data. This prevented smaller time-interval analysis and continuous monitoring.

To address this limitation, the project proposed a web scraping extension using Worldometer real-time statistics. The sample crawled data included indicator names, current values, and timestamps for health and expenditure indicators. This created a proof-of-concept for ongoing health and SDG monitoring.

Policy Implications

Promote equitable healthcare workforce distribution

Address regional disparities in healthcare staffing and access.

Align workforce planning with economic strategy

Consider healthcare capacity alongside GDP, urbanisation, and public expenditure.

Invest in tertiary education pathways

Support long-term healthcare workforce development through stronger education systems.

Build monitoring and escalation processes

Use real-time indicators to detect emerging health risks and support timely intervention.
