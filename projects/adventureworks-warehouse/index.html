AdventureWorks Product Performance Data Warehouse

A SQL Server BI project using star schema design, SSIS ETL, SSAS cube modelling, and Excel OLAP analysis to explore product sales, cost, revenue, and profitability.

Project Overview

This project analysed product performance in the AdventureWorks2012 database by building a structured BI pipeline rather than relying on ad hoc queries alone. The group designed a star schema with a central sales fact table and product, time, location, and sales order dimensions, then used SSIS transformations to prepare the dimension and fact tables.

The resulting SSAS cube enabled multidimensional analysis of revenue, cost, profit, quantity sold, sales channel, geography, product hierarchy, and time. The goal was to support product performance analysis across online and in-store channels, identify profitable and loss-making product categories, and translate cube-based analysis into sales and marketing recommendations.

BI Workflow

The project followed a full business intelligence workflow, moving from source data modelling to ETL, cube creation, and business-facing analysis.

01

Define Business Questions

Focused the analysis on product popularity, profitability, sales channels, promotion opportunities, and offline performance by country and month.

02

Design Star Schema

Structured the warehouse around a central sales fact table linked to product, time, location, and sales order dimensions.

03

Build SSIS Transformations

Prepared dimension and fact tables, handled missing values, transformed coded fields into readable labels, and calculated revenue, cost, and profit fields.

04

Create SSAS Cube

Built a multidimensional cube with measures such as revenue, cost, profit, quantity sold, revenue percentage, and cost percentage.

05

Analyse Cube in Excel

Used Excel PivotTables to browse the cube and compare product performance across channel, time, geography, and product hierarchy.

06

Translate Findings

Converted cube outputs into product, channel, and promotion recommendations for sales and marketing decision-making.

Business Questions

Question 1

Which products are most or least popular in-store and online?

The cube was used to compare product popularity by quantity sold and examine how sales volume related to revenue and cost across different sales channels.

Question 2

Which products are most or least profitable in-store and online?

Profit, revenue, and cost measures were compared to identify products where high revenue did not necessarily translate into strong profitability.

Question 3

Which products should be targeted with sales and marketing promotions?

The analysis compared profitability by product and channel to recommend where promotions could create the strongest business value.

Question 4

How do offline products perform across countries and months?

The cube was used to explore country-level and month-level patterns in offline profitability across product categories.

Key Outcomes

Popularity did not always mean profitability

Some high-volume in-store products generated strong revenue but also carried high costs, reducing or reversing profit outcomes.

Online sales showed stronger profitability

Online products generally had better cost control, meaning profitable product lines performed more effectively through online channels.

Promotion focus should consider channel performance

The analysis supported online promotions for Mountain Bikes, Road Bikes, and Touring Bikes, while recommending caution for loss-making in-store products.

Offline performance varied by country and category

Canada showed stronger in-store profitability, while Components and Accessories were more consistently profitable across countries than Bikes.
